# Text and Message Writing

## Purpose

Write a brief message that sounds natural in an existing relationship.

## Required inputs

Determine who is receiving it, what happened, the desired response, and whether the tone is casual, warm, firm, apologetic, or professional.

## Hard constraints

- Do not invent shared history, availability, or commitments.
- Avoid email conventions such as subject lines and formal sign-offs unless requested.
- Preserve boundaries and refusals clearly.

## Soft guidance

Contractions, sentence fragments, and light punctuation are allowed. Do not over-explain a simple message or manufacture enthusiasm.

## Research and citations

Research is rarely appropriate. If factual lookup is needed, keep sources in metadata rather than the message.

## Pre-delivery checklist

Read it as a phone notification. Confirm the intent is obvious, the length is proportionate, and the wording does not imply an unintended promise.
